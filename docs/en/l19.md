::: danger Read this first
Futures use high leverage, and losses can exceed the money you put in. The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) warns that many people lose all their money trading futures and can be required to pay more. This chapter helps you understand futures; it doesn't suggest beginners trade them.
:::

## What a futures contract is

A **futures contract** is a standardized agreement: buyer and seller agree to trade a set amount of an asset on a future date at a price fixed today.

Compared with options:

| | Options | Futures |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Has a right, no obligation | Has an obligation |
| Seller | Has an obligation | Has an obligation |
| Paid to open | Buyer pays a premium | Both sides post margin (Lesson 20) |
| Maximum loss | Buyer loses at most the premium | Both sides can lose a lot |

Futures trade on exchanges, with quantity, quality and expiration months set by the contract. The largest US futures exchange operator is CME Group.

- **Buying** futures is going **long**: you profit if the price rises.
- **Selling** futures is going **short**: you profit if the price falls. You don't need to own the asset first.

## Hedging and speculation

Futures began as a fix for a real problem: farmers feared grain prices would fall by harvest, and millers feared they would rise. Agreeing on a price in advance let both run their businesses.

Hypothetical numbers: it's March, spot wheat is $6 a bushel and September wheat futures are also $6.

| Wheat price in September | Farmer (sold futures) | Mill (bought futures) |
|---|---|---|
| Falls to $5 | Gets $1 less for wheat, gains $1 on futures: effectively sells at $6 | Pays $1 less for wheat, loses $1 on futures: effectively buys at $6 |
| Rises to $7 | Gets $1 more for wheat, loses $1 on futures: effectively sells at $6 | Pays $1 more for wheat, gains $1 on futures: effectively buys at $6 |

Either way, both have locked in $6. That's **hedging**: using futures to offset a price risk you already have. The cost is giving up the extra gain if prices move your way.

**Speculation** means having no such risk and simply betting on price moves. Speculators take on risk and give hedgers someone to trade with.

## Delivery and rolling

At expiration, a futures contract settles one of two ways:

- **Physical delivery**: the seller delivers and the buyer takes delivery. Crude oil, grains and many other commodity futures work this way.
- **Cash settlement**: no goods change hands; the difference is paid in cash. For example, CME's E-mini S&P 500 futures expire quarterly (March, June, September, December) and settle in cash to the S&P 500 index on the third Friday of the expiration month.

Speculators don't want to make or take delivery, so they **close out** before expiration. To keep a position, they sell the expiring contract and buy the next month's, which is called **rolling**.

Rolling has a cost. If later months are priced higher than the near month, each roll means selling low and buying high. That's why commodity funds that hold futures can, over time, return very differently from the commodity's spot price.

### Negative oil in 2020

In April 2020, the pandemic crushed demand for oil and storage space ran short. On April 20, the day before the May WTI contract expired, its price fell from $17.73 a barrel to settle at **−$37.63**, according to the CFTC. The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) said it was the first time WTI futures had gone negative since trading began in 1983.

A negative price means holders had to pay someone to take their contracts. The contract was about to expire, and holders with no place to store oil couldn't take delivery, so they had to get out first.

The lesson: futures prices depend not only on the asset but also on contract rules, delivery and storage.

## Common futures markets

| Category | Examples | Common uses |
|---|---|---|
| Equity index | E-mini S&P 500, Micro E-mini S&P 500 | Institutions adjust stock exposure or hedge stock risk |
| Energy | WTI crude oil, natural gas | Energy companies and airlines hedge prices |
| Metals | Gold, silver, copper | Miners and users hedge; also investment demand |
| Interest rates | US Treasury futures | Banks and funds manage rate risk |
| Agriculture | Corn, wheat, soybeans | Farmers and food companies hedge |

Equity index futures trade nearly around the clock. CME's E-mini S&P 500 futures trade Sunday through Friday, 5 p.m. to 4 p.m. US Central Time the next day.

Trading futures requires a futures account at a broker and separate approval.

## Summary

- A futures contract is standardized: it fixes today a price for a future trade, and **both sides are obligated**.
- **Hedging** offsets a price risk you already have; **speculation** bets on price moves.
- Futures settle by physical delivery or in cash; speculators usually close out or roll before expiration.
- WTI's negative settlement in 2020 shows that delivery and storage rules can drive futures prices.
