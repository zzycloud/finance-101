## Margin is a deposit, not a down payment

When you buy stock, you pay in full. When you trade futures, you put up only a small amount as **margin**, also called a performance bond. It isn't a down payment on anything; it's a deposit to make sure you can cover daily losses.

- **Initial margin**: the minimum you must deposit to open a position.
- **Maintenance margin**: the minimum you must keep in the account, usually below initial margin.

Exchanges and brokers set margin levels and adjust them as markets move.

## Daily settlement

A futures account is settled every day at the closing price, which is called **marking to market**. Gains are added to your account that day; losses are taken out that day.

Hypothetical numbers: the Micro E-mini S&P 500 contract is **$5 × the S&P 500 index**. You buy one with the index at 6,000, so the contract is worth $30,000. Assume initial margin of $2,000 and maintenance margin of $1,800 (actual amounts are set by the exchange and your broker).

| | Index | Day's P/L | Account balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | 6,000 | | $2,000 |
| Day 1 | 6,060 | +$300 | $2,300 |
| Day 2 | 5,950 | −$550 | $1,750, below $1,800, must top up to $2,000 |
| Day 3 | 5,700 | −$1,250 | $750 |

From 6,000 to 5,700, the index fell only 5%, yet the contract lost $1,500, or 75% of the initial margin.

## How leverage magnifies

Controlling a $30,000 contract with $2,000 of margin is 15-to-1 leverage.

| Index move | Contract P/L | Versus $2,000 margin |
|---|---|---|
| +2% | +$600 | +30% |
| −2% | −$600 | −30% |
| −5% | −$1,500 | −75% |
| −7% | −$2,100 | −105%, more than the margin |

A 7% one-day drop is rare but has happened. As Lesson 10 noted, the 7% Level 1 circuit breaker was triggered four times in less than two weeks in March 2020. Futures trade almost around the clock, so big overnight moves hit your account directly.

## Margin calls and forced liquidation

If your balance falls below maintenance margin, you get a **margin call** and must bring the account back up to initial margin by a deadline.

If you don't pay in time, or the market moves too fast, your broker will close your position:

- The broker **may not tell you first** or wait for you to pay.
- The closing price can be bad, near the low of the move.
- If losses exceed your account balance, **you still owe the difference**. The CFTC warns that futures traders can be required to pay more than they put in.

## Stock margin accounts work the same way

Leverage isn't only in futures. A broker **margin account** lets you borrow to buy stock. According to the SEC's investor.gov:

- Under the Federal Reserve's Regulation T, you may borrow up to **50%** of the purchase price.
- FINRA requires maintenance of at least **25%** of the market value of the securities; many brokers require more.
- Your broker can sell your securities without contacting you first, and you can lose more than you invested.

Example: you buy $10,000 of stock with $5,000 of your own money and $5,000 borrowed.

| Stock move | Market value | Your equity | Your return |
|---|---|---|---|
| +20% | $12,000 | $7,000 | +40% |
| −20% | $8,000 | $3,000 | −40% |
| About −33% | $6,667 | $1,667 (25% of value) | About −67%, at the 25% maintenance level |
| −60% | $4,000 | −$1,000 | −120%; you owe the broker $1,000 |

You also pay interest on the loan, which adds to your cost even if the stock doesn't move.

## Reading contract specs

Before trading any futures contract, look up its contract specs on the exchange's website. CME's Micro E-mini S&P 500 as an example:

| Item | Value | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Contract unit | $5 × S&P 500 index | Contract value = index × $5 |
| Minimum tick | 0.25 index points | Each tick = 0.25 × $5 = $1.25 |
| Settlement | Cash | No physical delivery |
| Expirations | Quarterly | When you must close or roll |

Turn the contract unit into dollars per point, then ask: if the index falls 5% or 10%, how much do you lose, and can your account take it?

## Summary

- Futures margin is a performance deposit, not a down payment; the account is **settled daily**.
- Leverage cuts both ways: a small move can wipe out most of your margin.
- After a margin call, the broker can close your position; losses can **exceed** what you deposited.
- Stock margin accounts carry the same risk: Reg T lets you borrow up to 50%; FINRA maintenance is at least 25%.
- Read the contract specs and work out your dollars per point before trading.
