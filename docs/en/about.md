# About this course

**Finance 101** is a free, open-source course for complete beginners, focused on US markets, in Chinese and English.

## Why it exists

A lot of personal-finance content is either too technical, full of jargon, or too hyped, all "get rich quick" and "this can't lose". We want a course that **explains things like a friend would**, **backs every number with a source** and **goes from easy to advanced**:

- It starts from "why invest at all" and assumes you know nothing.
- Every lesson has real numerical examples and a short quiz.
- It covers solid foundations first (funds, index investing), then complex tools (options, futures), with the risks spelled out.

## How to use it

1. Follow the [Learning Path](/en/) from Lesson 1, in order.
2. Take the quiz at the end of each lesson to check you really got it.
3. Click "Mark this lesson as done" when you finish; progress is saved in your browser.
4. Look up unfamiliar words in the [Glossary](/en/glossary).

## Important disclaimer

::: warning Please read
- Everything on this site is **for education only** and is not investment, tax or legal advice.
- Funds, tickers and companies mentioned are **examples**, not recommendations.
- Investing involves risk, including loss of principal. Past performance doesn't predict future results.
- Tax rules and contribution limits change every year. We cite official sources and note the year where we can, but check current official information before making decisions, and consult a licensed professional when needed.
:::

## Main sources

- IRS: retirement account limits and rules
- SEC's investor education site, Investor.gov
- FINRA
- SIPC
- Bureau of Labor Statistics: CPI data
- S&P Dow Jones Indices: SPIVA reports
- Federal Reserve G.19 consumer credit release (credit card rates)
- Yardeni Research: S&P 500 bear market statistics
- Fund issuers' websites (expense ratios)
- Vanguard research
