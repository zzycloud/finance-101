# Glossary

Common terms, grouped by topic. When you hit a word you don't know, look it up here. It grows as new lessons are added.

## Basics

- **Inflation**: prices rising across the economy, so the same money buys less. See [Lesson 1](/en/l01).
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**: the most common US inflation measure, published monthly by the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).
- **Purchasing power**: how much a sum of money can buy.
- **Nominal / real return**: return before inflation / after inflation.
- **Compound interest**: interest that earns interest of its own.
- **Rule of 72**: 72 ÷ annual return ≈ years to double.
- **Annualized return**: a multi-year return expressed as an average yearly rate.

## Risk

- **Volatility**: how much a price moves up and down. See [Lesson 2](/en/l02).
- **Drawdown**: the drop from a peak to the following low.
- **Diversification**: spreading money across many different assets to limit the damage from any one.
- **Time horizon**: how long until you need the money.
- **Risk tolerance**: how much loss you can handle, emotionally and financially.

## Getting ready

- **Emergency fund**: cash for surprises, usually 3–6 months of essential expenses. See [Lesson 3](/en/l03).
- **High-yield savings account**: a bank savings account paying a higher rate, usually FDIC-insured.
- **FDIC**: the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, insuring bank deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category.
- **Money market fund**: a fund holding short-term, high-quality debt; very stable, but not FDIC-insured.
- **APR**: the annual interest rate on a loan or credit card.

## Accounts

- **Broker / brokerage**: a firm that lets you buy and sell securities. See [Lesson 4](/en/l04).
- **SIPC**: the Securities Investor Protection Corporation, protecting customer assets within limits if a brokerage fails; it does not cover market losses.
- **Taxable brokerage account**: an investing account with no special tax treatment and no withdrawal limits.
- **401(k)**: an employer-sponsored retirement plan, often with a match.
- **Employer match**: money your employer adds in proportion to your contributions.
- **Vesting**: how long you must work before employer contributions fully belong to you.
- **IRA (Individual Retirement Account)**: a retirement account you open yourself; traditional or Roth.
- **Roth**: an account type taxed going in and tax-free coming out, if rules are met.
- **MAGI**: modified adjusted gross income, used to determine Roth IRA eligibility.
- **W-8BEN**: a form non-US persons use to certify their tax status to US financial institutions.

## Funds

- **Fund**: a pool of investors' money invested together by a professional manager. See [Lesson 5](/en/l05).
- **NAV (net asset value)**: the value of one share of a fund.
- **Mutual fund**: a fund bought and sold at the NAV calculated each trading day.
- **ETF (exchange-traded fund)**: a fund that trades on an exchange in real time, like a stock.
- **Expense ratio**: the yearly fee a fund charges as a percentage of assets.
- **Load**: a sales charge some mutual funds take when you buy or sell.
- **Active / passive fund**: a manager picks investments / the fund follows an index.
- **Index**: a rules-based group of securities used to measure a market. See [Lesson 6](/en/l06).
- **S&P 500**: a stock index of about 500 large US companies.
- **Market capitalization**: share price × shares outstanding.
- **Market-cap weighted**: bigger companies get bigger weights in the index.
- **SPIVA**: S&P Dow Jones Indices' report comparing active funds with their benchmarks.

## Trading and choosing funds

- **Ticker**: a security's short symbol, like VOO. See [Lesson 7](/en/l07).
- **AUM (assets under management)**: the total assets a fund manages.
- **Bid / ask**: the highest price a buyer will pay / the lowest a seller will accept.
- **Spread**: ask minus bid; a hidden trading cost.
- **Market order**: an order that fills immediately at the best available price.
- **Limit order**: an order that fills only at your price or better.
- **Tracking difference**: the gap between an index fund's return and its index's return.
- **Leveraged / inverse ETF**: an ETF seeking a multiple, or the opposite, of an index's daily return; resets daily and isn't meant for long-term holding.

## Portfolio management

- **Asset allocation**: how you split money among stocks, bonds and other assets. See [Lesson 8](/en/l08).
- **Three-fund portfolio**: US stocks, international stocks and bonds, using three index funds.
- **Target-date fund**: an all-in-one fund built around a retirement year that gets more conservative over time.
- **Glide path**: how a target-date fund lowers its stock share over time.
- **Dollar-cost averaging (DCA)**: investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule.
- **Lump-sum investing**: investing a whole sum at once.
- **Rebalancing**: bringing a portfolio back to its target mix.

## Stocks

- **Stock / share**: a unit of ownership in a company. See [Lesson 9](/en/l09).
- **IPO (initial public offering)**: a company's first sale of shares to the public and listing on an exchange.
- **Common / preferred stock**: common stock votes and has variable dividends; preferred usually has a fixed dividend and no vote.
- **Stop order**: becomes a market order once the stop price is reached. The stop price is not a guaranteed price. See [Lesson 10](/en/l10).
- **Stop-limit order**: becomes a limit order once the stop price is reached; the fill price has a floor, but it may not fill.
- **T+1 settlement**: trades settle one business day after the trade date. US stocks since May 28, 2024.
- **Market-wide circuit breaker**: a market-wide trading halt triggered when the S&P 500 falls 7%, 13% or 20% in a day.
- **Income statement / balance sheet / cash flow statement**: the three core financial statements. See [Lesson 11](/en/l11).
- **Earnings per share (EPS)**: net income ÷ shares outstanding.
- **Free cash flow**: operating cash flow minus capital expenditure.
- **10-K / 10-Q / 8-K**: annual, quarterly and major-event reports filed with the SEC, free on EDGAR.
- **Price-to-earnings (P/E)**: share price ÷ EPS. See [Lesson 12](/en/l12).
- **Price-to-book (P/B) / price-to-sales (P/S)**: price ÷ book value per share; market cap ÷ annual revenue.
- **Dividend yield**: annual dividend per share ÷ share price.
- **Payout ratio**: dividends ÷ net income.
- **Ex-dividend date**: buy on or after this day and you don't get this dividend. See [Lesson 13](/en/l13).
- **Buyback**: a company repurchases, and usually retires, its own shares.
- **Stock split / reverse split**: dividing each share into several, or combining several into one; total value is unchanged.
- **Dividend reinvestment (DRIP)**: dividends automatically buy more of the same stock or fund.
