::: tip Before you start
This lesson covers basic US federal tax rules, using IRS figures for **tax year 2026**. State taxes vary and tax law changes. Check current IRS rules for your situation and consult a tax professional when needed.
:::

## When you owe tax

Tax mostly applies in a **taxable brokerage account**. Inside tax-advantaged accounts such as IRAs and 401(k)s (Lesson 4), trades and dividends aren't taxed when they happen.

In a taxable account, three kinds of events are taxed:

1. **Selling at a gain**: capital gains.
2. **Receiving dividends**.
3. **Receiving interest**: from bonds and money market funds.

A holding that's up but unsold has an **unrealized gain**, which isn't taxed. Selling turns it into a **realized gain**.

## Capital gains: short- and long-term

**Capital gain = sale price − cost basis.**

| | Holding period | Tax rate |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term capital gain | One year or less | Ordinary graduated income tax rates |
| Long-term capital gain | More than one year | 0%, 15% or 20% |

Long-term rates for 2026 depend on taxable income:

| Rate | Single | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | Up to $49,450 | Up to $98,900 |
| 15% | Up to $545,500 | Up to $613,700 |
| 20% | Above $545,500 | Above $613,700 |

Higher earners also owe the **3.8% net investment income tax (NIIT)** when modified adjusted gross income exceeds $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).

**Example**: a $5,000 gain, assuming a 24% marginal rate on ordinary income.

| | Sold after 11 months | Sold after 13 months |
|---|---|---|
| Tax rate | 24% | 15% |
| Tax | $1,200 | $750 |

Waiting two more months saves $450. Holding for the long term helps with compounding and with taxes.

## Dividends: qualified and ordinary

| | Tax rate | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified dividends | Same as long-term gains: 0%, 15%, 20% | From US or qualified foreign companies, with the holding period met |
| Ordinary dividends | Ordinary income rates | Dividends that don't qualify |

The holding period: you must hold the stock **more than 60 days during the 121-day period that starts 60 days before the ex-dividend date**. Buy and sell quickly around the ex-dividend date and the dividend won't qualify.

Common cases:

- Dividends from large US companies and US stock index funds are mostly qualified.
- Bond fund distributions come mainly from interest and are taxed as ordinary income.
- Most REIT dividends aren't qualified.

## Using losses

Losses from sales reduce your tax:

1. They first offset capital gains in the same year.
2. Any net loss left offsets up to **$3,000** of ordinary income a year ($1,500 if married filing separately).
3. The rest **carries forward** to later years, with no expiration.

**Tax-loss harvesting**: in a taxable account, you sell a losing holding to realize the loss, then buy a similar but not substantially identical fund to stay invested. For example, sell an S&P 500 fund and buy a total market index fund.

### The wash sale rule

If you buy a **substantially identical** security **within 30 days before or after** selling at a loss, it's a **wash sale**, and the loss can't be deducted that year.

- The disallowed loss isn't gone; it's added to the cost basis of the new shares and shows up when you eventually sell them.
- The same stock or the same ETF is clearly substantially identical. Whether funds from different companies tracking the same index count isn't clearly defined by the IRS.
- Buying the same security back in an IRA can also trigger a wash sale.
- Automatic dividend reinvestment (Lesson 13) is a purchase too and can cause small wash sales.

## Form 1099

Early each year your broker sends **Form 1099**s, often combined into one document called a consolidated 1099:

| Form | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1099-B | Sales: proceeds, cost basis, short- or long-term, any wash sales |
| 1099-DIV | Dividends and fund distributions, with qualified dividends shown separately |
| 1099-INT | Interest income |

At tax time, enter these figures on your return or import them into tax software. Brokers sometimes issue corrected forms after the first ones; use the corrected version.

## Non-US residents

If you're not a US tax resident, the rules differ:

- When you open the account, you file **Form W-8BEN** to certify your status and claim any treaty benefits.
- US-source **dividends** are withheld at **30%** by default; a tax treaty between your country and the US can lower that. Under the US-China treaty, for example, the rate on ordinary dividends is **10%**.
- **Capital gains** from selling stock generally aren't taxed by the US if you're in the US fewer than 183 days in the year.
- Your home country may also tax this income; follow its rules.

## Summary

- In a taxable account, **gains on sales, dividends and interest** are taxed; unsold gains aren't.
- Held **more than one year**, gains are long-term, taxed at 0%/15%/20%; one year or less, at ordinary rates.
- **Qualified dividends** get long-term rates if the holding period is met.
- Net losses offset up to **$3,000** of ordinary income a year, with the rest carried forward; watch the **30-day wash sale rule**.
- Non-US residents file W-8BEN; dividends are withheld at 30% by default, lower under a tax treaty.
